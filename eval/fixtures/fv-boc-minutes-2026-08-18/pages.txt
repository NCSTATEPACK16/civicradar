     located at 210 S West Street, from the Residential Medium Density (RMD) Zoning
     District to the Town Center Residential (TCR) Zoning District.

     Staff Comments – Planning Director Pam Davison submitted the agenda abstract and
     supporting documents to the Town's official record. She then presented the attached
     staff report.

     Public Hearing – The public hearing was opened. Wayne Mauldin, 139 N. Main Street,
     spoke in favor of the zoning map amendment. Aymie Feeney, 609 Parker Street spoke
     in opposition to the zoning map amendment.

--- page 7 ---
Discussion – Commissioner Adcock emphasized that the success of this zoning type
is evident in the three-story units located at the corner of Spring Avenue and Vance
Street, which he described as significant assets to downtown Fuquay. He stressed
that increasing residential density is crucial for maintaining a vibrant downtown;
without a sufficient population base, he warned that local businesses could move to
major arterial roads. Additionally, Commissioner Adcock highlighted the importance
of providing diverse housing options while ensuring that developers are not unduly
burdened in an increasingly unaffordable market. Regarding the current proposal, he
noted that this type of development utilizes the existing lot for three units without
subdivisions, which is precisely what downtown Fuquay needs.

Commissioner Vorren concurred with Commissioner Adcock's assessment,
emphasizing that the property is located just two blocks from South Main Street.

Recommendation – Approve REZ-2026-10, a zoning map amendment for 210 S West
Street, from the Residential Medium Density (RMD) Zoning District to the Town
Center Residential (TCR) Zoning District, as it is reasonable and in the best interest
of the public for the reasons identified by management and staff.

Discussion – Commissioner Vorren noted that the Town Board has received
considerable feedback on the TCR zoning. He emphasized that the rapid growth of
Fuquay-Varina, with its current population of over 40,000 and an expected 50%
increase over the next decade, requires a careful approach. The Town Board must
thoughtfully consider both the pace of expansion and the specific location of this
neighborhood. Therefore, he requested that the item be tabled to allow for further
study and a comparative analysis of how neighboring towns are addressing similar
challenges.

Commissioner Haynes reported that, after extensive consultation with the
neighborhood, he is committed to conducting a transparent and balanced evaluation
of the proposal. He questioned whether there are alternative uses for the property,
which Planning Director Davison clarified are limited to single-family homes on
reduced setback lots, rather than duplexes, due to density concerns.

Commissioner Haynes noted that, although this request shares some similarities
with past TCR zoning issues in the Lincoln Heights area, the transition from two to
three lots makes it unique. Recognizing the complexities involved in maintaining
community character while treating all constituents fairly, Commissioner Haynes
requested to table the item until a comparative study of how other municipalities
handle similar zoning requests can be completed.

Commissioner Haynes emphasized the ongoing need for "missing middle" housing,
noting that rising property taxes are making it increasingly difficult for seniors and
other residents to afford their homes. He highlighted that addressing this affordability
gap is a priority for the Town Board. Additionally, he called for further efforts to
preserve the historic character of Fuquay-Varina. In response, Commissioner
Adcock clarified that while the neighborhood in question is adjacent to the

--- page 10 ---
recognized historic district—often referred to as the "historic fringe"—it does not
currently have an official historical designation.

Commissioner Smith mentioned that he has toured the neighborhood and met with
several residents. He highlighted the importance of evaluating the proposal in
relation to existing developments, previous approvals in other communities, and the
specific requirements outlined in the Town’s Land Use Plan. To make an informed
decision, he suggested tabling the request for a limited period to allow staff to
conduct comparative research into how similar municipalities address these issues.
Finally, Commissioner Smith expressed his support for the proposed plans and for
the design of the homes currently being built by Pine State.

Commissioner Adcock praised the residents who voiced their opposition,
highlighting their respectful demeanor and their wish for the Town Board to
collaborate in finding the best solution for the neighborhood. He recognized Pine
State's contributions to Fuquay-Varina's aesthetic and expressed pride in the high-
quality buildings made possible by TCR zoning. Commissioner Adcock emphasized
that the strength of TCR lies in its flexibility, which allows developers to present a final
product to the Town Board for approval. He prefers this approach over the existing
standard zoning.

Commissioner Adcock believes that TCR should not be eliminated, but he stressed
the importance of taking time to assess how the Town manages TCR in different
neighborhoods. He expressed hope that residents and the developer could come to
a mutually agreeable compromise and supported postponing the current request for
further consideration.

Mayor Pro-Tem Gardner shared her personal connection to the area, mentioning that
she lived there for 12 years and recently sold a property in downtown. She expressed

--- page 11 ---
her appreciation for the residents’ respectful testimony during the public hearing and
validated their descriptions of what makes living in the downtown area unique.
Additionally, she agreed with her fellow commissioners on the importance of
preserving the neighborhood’s traditional character and historic charm. Finally, she
acknowledged Pine State for its high-quality work throughout the Town of Fuquay-
Varina.

Commissioner Haynes invited the public to share their thoughts on the future vision
for the area. He noted that the Town Board is actively exploring ways to improve
connections between the downtown districts of Fuquay and Varina. He stressed the
importance of establishing safer navigation routes between the two communities,
specifically proposing alternative paths from the Fuquay side to avoid a direct route
down Main Street. Commissioner Haynes highlighted the significant long-term
opportunities to enhance pedestrian safety and connectivity toward Ennis Street on
the Varina side. He encouraged residents to continue offering feedback on these
infrastructure goals, as it will help guide future planning efforts.

7E   Zoning Map Amendment - M/I Homes of Raleigh, LLC - 0, 6921, & a portion of 6823
     Sunset Lake Road - PINs 0667674582, 0667681140 & a portion of 0667589880 -
     REZ-2025-18

     Purpose – To consider a requested zoning map amendment for a total of 27.63 acres,
     located at 0, 6921 & a portion of 6823 Sunset Lake Road from the Residential Medium
     Density Conditional Zoning District (RMD-CZD) to the Planned Unit Development
     (PUD) Zoning District.

--- page 14 ---
Staff Comments – Planning Director Pam Davison submitted the agenda abstract and
supporting documents to the Town's official record. She then presented the attached
staff report.

Public Hearing – The public hearing was opened. Juan Montes, 1511 Sunday Drive,
Raleigh, spoke in favor of the zoning map amendment. Wendy Douglas, 5005 Hanna
Lane spoke in opposition to the zoning map amendment.

Discussion – Commissioner Adcock asked about the potential effects of the historic
district designation on the property. Planning Director Davison clarified that this
designation does not impose any restrictions, emphasizing that participation in the
historic district is voluntary. She also explained that Ballentine Farms, which is part
of the district, remains a subdivision without any applicable standards or limitations.

Commissioner Adcock asked about NCDOT project U5751, highlighting that
relocating certain residences was necessary to keep them outside the historic
district. Planning Director Davison explained that, since participation in the historic
district is voluntary, there are no guarantees regarding specific protections or
standards for properties within the area.

Commissioner Adcock raised concerns that listing a property on the National
Register of Historic Places might limit the Town's ability to make infrastructure
improvements, such as upgrading roads. He specifically mentioned a perceived
imbalance in the Town's authority, suggesting that it may be more restrictive
regarding historic properties during private sales than for other types of properties.
Planning Director Davison clarified that, to her knowledge, historic designations are
voluntary.

--- page 15 ---
Commissioner Adcock asked whether there is a formal consultation process with the
Historic Commission when a building is designated as part of the National Historic
District. Planning Director Davison replied that while staff can request feedback, no
historic buildings have been identified on this property to date. She gave an example
from the Village of Lake Wheeler, where Capital Area Preservation and the State
Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) were involved in renovating a historic house and
creating a separate lot. Planning Director Davison emphasized that if any significant
structures are identified, staff will work with both Capital Area Preservation and the
State Historic Preservation Office to ensure their preservation. She also clarified that,
since the current development is limited to the southern portion of the property, no
existing historical structures are expected to be impacted, as most buildings are
located on the northern side, which is not part of the current project.

Commissioner Adcock requested that Capital Area Preservation evaluate the
property to identify any significant historical structures. Planning Director Davison
responded that the staff would assist with this inquiry. She noted that while the
preliminary subdivision plans have already undergone an initial review, the current
plan includes removing existing brick and concrete foundations and demolishing
several remaining buildings.

In response to Commissioner Adcock's inquiries, Juan Montes stated that a formal
report concerning the existing structures has been submitted to SHPO. According to
this report, there are no specific historical features on the site that require
preservation. Mr. Montes expressed his willingness to coordinate a site visit with the
Town's museum board and offered to donate any significant historical materials or
artifacts of interest.

--- page 16 ---
Commissioner Adcock asked if the Town had received the report from SHPO. Mr.
Montes replied that he would deliver a copy of the report directly to Planning Director
Davison.

Commissioner Adcock provided background on the property’s history and
recommended that Mr. Montes arrange a meeting with representatives from the
Fuquay-Varina Museum to ensure that no significant historical features are
overlooked. Mr. Montes expressed his willingness to meet with these representatives
and clarified that most historic features are located on the northern portion of the
property, specifically on Ms. Caroline Elliot’s parcel. In response to Commissioner
Adcock's inquiry about whether her residence is within the proposed rezoning area,
Mr. Montes confirmed that it is not, noting that she is a co-owner and is only selling
the barn acreage. Commissioner Adcock reiterated that he does not oppose
residential development; however, given the property's designation on the National
Register of Historic Places, he emphasized the importance of conducting thorough
due diligence to ensure that no historical elements are omitted from the planning
process.

Commissioner Adcock highlighted the unique character of Fuquay-Varina,
emphasizing that the property's dairy history is a significant part of its distinction. In
response, Mr. Montes proposed preserving this heritage by installing a
commemorative plaque or another form of historical marker on the property.

Commissioner Adcock asked about the proposed name for the subdivision. Mr.
Montes mentioned that while "Galloway Crossing" has been considered, the final
choice will be made by his marketing team. In response to a suggestion for a dairy-
themed name, Mr. Montes clarified that research indicates dairy operations were not
located on this specific part of the property; it was primarily used as a horse barn. He
explained that the entrance monument will feature a horse-themed design.

      significant contributing feature to its heritage status.
