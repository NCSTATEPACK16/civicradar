Type: Regular Item
Title: Athens Drive Library Site Options
Matter name: Ashley Jacobs, County Manager’s Office
Note: Ms. Ashley Jacobs, Deputy County Manager, shared the Athens Drive Library Site Options.

She provided a recap of the information shared with the Commissioners during their March 9, 2026 Work Session regarding potential site options for the Athens Drive Library. She noted that more than 115 sites were evaluated for the new location, and that list was ultimately narrowed to two: the Well Fed Garden site and the Tryon Road site.

She explained that, at the conclusion of that presentation, the Board directed staff to begin discussions with the property owners of the Well Fed Garden site and to initiate negotiations on a potential purchase price.

She shared that today’s presentation will include an update on those negotiations, as well as new information regarding public transit access to the Tryon Road site. Ms. Jacobs will provide a refresher on the site selection criteria, present the new information, and then ask the Board for direction on how staff should proceed in preparing items for the June 15, 2026 Regular Meeting agenda.

She shared that the goal is for residents to be within a 10-minute drive of the new library site. She also presented a chart outlining the regional, community, specialty, and future community library categories.

She shared Athens Drive Search Area:
*4-5 acres is typical for community branch
*Prefer 7-8 acres for future expansion, if needed
*Appropriate zoning
*Good transportation access
*Good visibility from the road
*Prefer to own the land and building

She shared Athens Drive Search Area:
*Initial Search included 115 sites
*Search Criteria:
-Drive Time Proximity
-Transit Proximity
-Safe Accessibility to Site
-Neighborhood Walkability
-Access to Marginalized Populations
-Zoning Compatibility
-Adequate Acreage
-Site Character - Topo/Site Geometry
-Utility Infrastructure Access
-Acquisition Cost
-Development Cost
-Expandability to Regional
-Availability

She shared a map of Best Options for Well Fed Garden and Tryon Road.

She shared Well Fed Garden:
*Across the street from current library location
*Site is very tight (2.6 acres), difficult/expensive to develop
*Two-story library likely
*Not expandable in the future
*Must maintain driveway access to rear adjacent property (not for sale)
*$1.8M asking price

She shared new information on Well Fed Garden - New Information:
*Staff proceeded as directed by the Board at the March work session
*Owner and County appraisals completed
*Agreed-upon purchase price $1.586M
*Letter of Intent signed

She shared new information on Tryon Road:
*Twelve acres - surplus WCPSS property
*Easily accommodates future expansion and/or other uses such as affordable housing
*Close to Swift Creek ES
*Town of Cary jurisdiction
*Acquisition terms TBD; possible land swap with WCPSS

She shared Tryon Road - New Information:
-GoTriangle Board has approved a realignment of Bus Route 305, effective Aug. 2, 2026
-Route will run hourly from 6 a.m.- 9 a.m. on weekdays and 7 a.m.- 7 p.m. on Sundays
-Nearest stop to Tryon Road site is on GoCary Route 1, about 1/3 of a mile away, and the nearest stop on GoTriangle Route 305 is about 2/3 of a mile away

She shared the Tryon Road Site Bus Service map.
She shared the Tryon Road Site Bus Service (GoTriangle and GoCary).
She shared GoTriangle shared they would consider adding a bus stop if the library was placed on Tryon Road - this would be done in 2027.

She shared Tryon Road - New Information:
*WCPSS purchased the site in 2019.
*Due diligence studies were performed, including preliminary geotechnical and a Phase 1 Environmental
*Site was annexed, and rezoning was approved by the Town of Cary in 2022.

She shared a chart of 2025 Demographics.
She shared a chart of Primary Library for Active Cardholders (2024) Within Athens Drive Library 10-Minute Drive Region.

She shared a map of Title 1 Schools Proximity to sites (highlighted in yellow).

Commissioner Adamson asked for clarification on where Athens Drive High School was located on the map presented by Ms. Jacobs.

Ms. Jacobs pointed this out for Commissioner Adamson.

Ms. Jacobs shared that concluded her presentation and staff are seeking direction from the Board on a decision for site selection for the June 15, 2026 Regular Meeting agenda.

Commissioner Adamson noted that this is a topic the Board has discussed several times and acknowledged that it involves many difficult decisions. She shared that, in her view, the decision ultimately comes down to two primary criteria, with the first being the importance of considering the county as a whole.

She explained that, historically, some Wake County libraries have been too small to meet community needs. She cited the Green Road Library as an example, noting that it primarily serves an immigrant community and that, had the property not allowed for expansion, significant needs would have gone unmet. She also referenced the Southeast Raleigh Library, which had been in a leased space for many years without the ability to expand, resulting in community needs that could not be fully addressed. She added that the County now has two buildings capable of meeting those needs.

Commissioner Adamson stated that she supports the Tryon Road site because it offers room for expansion should the community’s needs grow or change in the future. She shared that the site is located near several elementary schools. Given recent cost-saving measures by WCPSS, including the reduction of early literacy coaches, she believes the Tryon Road location will help support Title I early literacy programs.

Commissioner Waters asked for clarification on the chart showing bus service. She inquired how someone traveling from either the current Athens Drive Library location or the proposed Well Fed Garden site would take the bus to reach the proposed Tryon Road location.

Ms. Jacobs explained that bus route 305 and the service along Avent Ferry Road are the closest options for the Athens Drive community. She shared that a rider would take route 305 and make a connection on Trailwood Drive. She also provided another example illustrating service near the intersection of Tryon Road and Dillard Drive.

Commissioner Waters asked how far the walk would be from the bus stop to the library. She also asked whether the stops located along the perimeter are situated on the Avent Ferry Road trail.

Ms. Jacobs shared the rider would walk two-thirds of a mile from the bus stop to library. She shared the rider would walk to Avent Ferry Road from the bus stop.

Commissioner Waters shared that, based on the demographics and key metrics for a 10-minute drive time, the chart indicates that relocating the library would reduce the number of residents within that radius by approximately 25,000-in effect, moving to a larger site while serving fewer residents within the 10-minute metric. She expressed concern about this decline and recalled past advocacy efforts to prioritize library access. She thanked Commissioner Adamson for highlighting the situations at the Green Road Library and the Southgate Library and shared the importance of securing those locations for expansion. She stated that seeing a reduction for 25,000 residents with convenient access is troubling to her, noting that access and resources vary across communities. For these reasons, she shared support for the Well Fed Garden site, as she does not want to see fewer people served.

Chair Mial acknowledge that Janet Cowell, Mayor, City of Raleigh is present at today's Work Session.

Commissioner Evans acknowledged that the Board has been reviewing potential sites for nearly three years and making difficult decisions along the way. She thanked staff for their diligence and thorough work throughout the process and the input received by the community on this matter. She shared that she had hoped a site closer to Athens Drive could have been identified and noted that staff had explored options with the school system to determine whether any adjacent properties might be feasible. Now, with the options narrowed back to two sites, she believes there are pros and cons to both.

Commissioner Evans shared that both sites are located in her district, and her priority is determining how best to serve the entire region with a long-term, forward-looking plan. Although the project is technically a replacement of the Athens Drive library, she shared that the decision must address the needs of the broader community for years to come.

Commissioner Evans shared regarding the Well Fed Garden site, she acknowledged its walkability but noted that the property is not optimally sized and does not allow for future expansion. She stated that building a two-story library on that site would result in less space for more cost. While walkability is valuable for a neighborhood library, she expressed concern about locating the facility within a residential area. She shared her hope that the eventual library site will attract strong community use.

Commissioner Evans then discussed the Tryon Road site, noting that it is larger, allows for expansion, and is highly visible-factors she believes could increase usage. She shared that public transportation is important to her and recalled asking last August whether the City of Raleigh could consider running a bus route into the edge of Cary. GoCary is evaluating additional routes as a priority, and she noted that GoTriangle already runs service from Holly Springs, Apex to Cary to downtown Raleigh. She expressed confidence that, based on planning efforts that begin in August, public transportation could be prioritized if the Tryon site is selected. A bus plan review will occur as part of the Wake Transit Plan, which will help guide these decisions.

Commissioner Evans stated that she would not support a site that does not serve the entire region. She acknowledged the demographic points raised by Commissioner Waters but noted that growing density, including new buildings and town homes, will be closer to the Yates Mill area. She has received feedback from residents concerned about the library remaining in Raleigh rather than moving into Cary. She clarified that the goal is not to build another library for Cary, but rather to ensure usable space that effectively serves the region.

Commissioner Evans shared that she has begun hearing from residents in other parts of the community, including Swift Creek areas who view the Yates Mill site as an attractive location. She shared the importance of planning for growth and reaching as many residents as possible. While she does not believe either site is perfect, she shared that no vote will be taken during Work Session. She concluded by sharing that she is currently leaning toward the Tryon Yates Mill site.

Vice Chair Jackson shared for the Title 1 schools proximity, what is known about the amount of students that use libraries.

Ms. Jacobs does not have that information.

Vice Chair Jackson shared that proximity is often used with the assumption that students will use the library closest to them, but she pointed out that there are no metrics to confirm this. She acknowledged that, despite the Athens Drive Library having limited hours and operational constraints due to its location within a high school, it still draws a high number of patrons-so much so that its usage is bolded in the chart alongside the Oberlin and Cary regional libraries. She then asked about the current capacity of the Oberlin and Cary regional libraries and whether those facilities are able to accommodate potential growth.

Ms. Jacobs shared that both the Oberlin and Cary Regional libraries are capable of serving the surrounding population. She added that the new library is expected to draw increased interest, offering an improved facility that will likely generate a higher level of activity.

Vice Chair Jackson shared a community library is drawing numbers as high as it is and that is important. She shared for the 10-Minute drive time information, she shared there is something for a community library being in the center of multiple regional libraries and presenting residents with a quant library experience and a choice with a regional library. She shared the projected growth in this area can put pressure on the other libraries, such that the new library has to be a regional library to account for additional growth.

Ms. Jacobs shared that expandability is always a key goal when building libraries, given Wake County’s ongoing growth. She acknowledged that Commissioner Evans is correct in noting the significant development occurring in the Tryon Road area, and she agreed it is helpful to have the ability to expand, if the new library were located at that site.

Vice Chair Jackson acknowledged, that is the challenge and balance of it all. She shared having regional libraries that can sustain additional growth, to build now, with the intention of growing, means the county is giving up the commitment to having a small, intimate community library, which having those types of libraries are valuable. She shared there are pros and cons at each proposed site and the need for the Board to give direction based on what is being heard from the community but also where the county's values sit.

Commissioner Stallings shared thanks to her colleagues for their input. She agreed that there are alot of pros and cons to each site. She thanked Ms. Jacobs for the work that she and staff have done. She shared appreciation to members of the community for sharing their support for the Athens Drive library to remain in the community. She asked Ms. Jacobs for clarity that if the new library is at the Well Fed Gardens site, would the be expensive to build, would this cost be beyond the county's budget that is allocated for the library bond.

Ms. Jacobs confirmed that the cost can be accommodated.

Commissioner Stallings asked what would a land swap look like.

Ms. Lucas, Deputy County Manager, shared that the county and WCPSS have jointly agreed to evaluate land that is not suitable for government purposes, as well as WCPSS properties that are not being used for educational purposes. The goal is to identify sites that could be exchanged to better support each entity’s needs.

Ms. Lucas shared the Tryon Road site may be suitable for a county office, affordable housing, or a combination of affordable housing and a library. She also shared that a proposed list of potential swap sites will be presented to the Commissioners later this fall.

Commissioner Stallings asked what are Title 1 schools and how are they defined and what is the student population of those that live close and those that are transported. She also asked about environmental considerations on the Tryon Road site.

Mr. Forestieri shared that, based on his evaluation of the site for potential school system purchase, there were no factors in the study that would have prevented a school from being built there. He shared there should likewise be no limitations on placing a library on the site. He explained that the necessary infrastructure is already in place and that nothing in the site conditions would preclude adding a library. He shared that while the property is located within a watershed, the evaluation considered impervious surface limits, and those restrictions would not prevent a library from being constructed.

County Manager Ellis shared definition of a Title 1 school, which is a public school that receives federal funding and is a component of the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA). He shared the WCPSS Title 1 department provides financial assistance and program support to high numbers of children from low-income families. All Title 1 eligible schools in Wake County School system operates under a model. Title 1 funds may be used with other school funding sources to provide resources and instructional support to help all children meet challenging academic standards.

Commissioner Stallings thanked County Manager for the explanation. She is trying to determine how many students within the Athens Drive community, that attend a Title 1 school versus those who might be transported.

Commissioner Adamson asked about the size of the Tryon Road site.

Ms. Jacobs shared the Tryon Road site is 12 acres.

Commissioner Adamson shared her observation that the Well Fed Garden 10-minute drive-time area extends all the way to Crabtree. She shared that just beyond Crabtree are the North Regional and Oberlin libraries. For residents living between the Well Fed Garden site and Crabtree, she explained it would be just as easy to travel to the Oberlin Library as to the Well Fed Garden site. She shared that Cary Regional, shown to the left on the displayed map, also falls within the 10-minute drive-time area, meaning residents would have access to multiple library locations. In contrast, she shared that residents near the Tryon Road site would have only one available library option.

Commissioner Waters noted that for Title I students, it is not uncommon for students to be assigned to schools that are not proximate to their homes, which can make it difficult for students to feel connected to their community. Although she did not have data to verify her remarks, she wanted to speak to it anecdotally.

Commissioner Waters asked how much evaluation has been done regarding the feasibility of a one-story versus a two-story building at the Well Fed Garden site. She also asked about the amount of space required for the property previously purchased for the Southgate Library. In addition, she asked whether libraries can be constructed as one-story buildings.

Mr. Forestieri explained that this is a preliminary review of the property. He shared that nothing is currently being developed at the Southgate site, which is 2.4 acres and already includes parking and shared parking. Because the property is within the City of Raleigh, stormwater management requirements apply, and a designated stormwater area was already in place at Southgate. He shared that the Well Fed Garden site has more available land, and the goal of providing approximately 90 parking spaces would increase the impervious surface area. He shared that the property must maintain an easement driveway to the landlocked parcel (2.6 acres).

Commissioner Waters asked if assessments have been preliminary.

Mr. Forestieri confirmed that is correct. He shared there is only two libraries with less than 3 acres (Wendell and the Harrison library). He shared the Harrison library only has 30 parking spaces and there is no opportunity for expansion in the future.

Commissioner Waters shared regarding the bus routes, she asked Ms. Jacobs if there was any information on existing transportation that currently serves the Well Fed Garden site and frequency of proposed bus changes for route 305.

Ms. Jacobs shared the realigned bus route 305 will run hourly from 6 a.m.- 9 a.m. on weekdays and 7 a.m.- 7 p.m. on Sundays.

Commissioner Waters asked specifically for the route for patrons to go to the Well Fed Garden site.

Ms. Jacobs shared she did not have any additional information regarding the existing route that would bring patrons to the Well Fed Garden proposed site.

Commissioner Thomas shared there has been discussion about the future expansion on the Tryon Road proposed site. She asked what would those potential expansion opportunities be.

Ms. Jacobs shared she is not familiar with any discussion of future projects for the Tryon Road proposed site

Mr. Forestieri shared to his knowledge, there has not been any other discussion of possible expansion projects for the Tryon Road site. He shared with the land being 12 acres, there is the possibility of expanding the library into a regional one.

Commissioner Thomas shared if the Tryon Road site is chosen, is there an opportunity to do both/and, such as a library and affordable housing or must it be one or the other.

Mr. Forestieri shared potentially, it can be both; but this has not been evaluated in detail to confirm.

Vice Chair Jackson asked for clarification that if the Well Fed Garden site is selected, that would leave three community libraries that are not able to expand.

Mr. Forestieri confirmed that the Harrison Library cannot be expanded. He shared that the Wendell Library is being relocated, and the Southgate Library will be either a regional facility or comparable in size to a regional library. He explained that the Middle Creek Library is somewhat landlocked and is likely not expandable. He also shared that the Wake Forest Library has already been expanded. He added that if Vice Chair Jackson’s question relates to the potential for a library to become a regional library, that process is more limiting. He shared that the Fuquay-Varina site is the smallest location that could feasibly be expanded into a regional library.

Vice Chair Jackson shared understanding that there would be a handful of community libraries that currently exist and the county can count on those libraries remaining community libraries in the future, simply by default of being landlocked.

Mr. Forestieri shared that is correct.

Vice Chair Jackson shared regarding the landswap, if the Well Fed Garden site is selected, does that preclude the county from engaging with the WCPSS board on landswaps for building affordable housing projects.

Mr. Forestieri, shared it would not preclude the county from engaging in landswaps with WCPSS.

Commissioner Waters asked if there is any information regarding the seller of Well Fed Garden.

Ms. Jacobs shared to her knowledge, the seller of the Well Fed Garden is still interested in selling the property.

Ms. Lucas shared regarding the question about the bus route that currently runs by Athens Drive, it is Route 11L and it currently runs every 40-45 minutes; 7am - 10pm weekdays; 7:45 to 10pm on Sundays.

Commissioner Evans shared that is the route that she was told by the City of Raleigh that they may consider moving a stop into that Crossroads area and it is possible that this would be the route to service that area. She asked that persons do not get locked into the GoTriangle route was the only option. She shared the City of Raleigh does have interest in getting service to this general area and all of that would be reviewed in terms of the bus plan.

Commissioner Stallings shared that the Commissioners asked staff to get an appraisal, and staff completed that request. She shared the price has been negotiated down and a letter of intent has been received.

The Commissioners shared their suggestions on the two proposed location options: Well Fed Garden and Tryon Road:

Commissioner Adamson - Tryon Road
Commissioner Waters- Well Fed Garden
Commissioner Evans - Tryon Road
Commissioner Stallings - Well Fed Garden (and would like to see county advocate for affordable housing on the Tryon Road property)
Commissioner Thomas - Well Fed Garden
Vice Chair Jackson - Well Fed Garden
Chair Mial - Well Fed Garden

Vice Chair Jackson acknowledged that the county going in this direction, she believes the county will be building a walkable, accessible library for the residents of that community. She shared the county does not have to turn away from the possibility of Tryon Road, and what it can bring for broader strategic goals around affordable housing. She shared appreciation for hearing that negotiations will continue to explore landswap options. She shared belief that this direction allows the county to maximize the benefits of both sites.

Commissioner Adamson asked if this item will be on consent or regular agenda. She shared this item was on the regular agenda. She would prefer to keep it on regular agenda as she will be voting no.

Chair Mial confirmed the Athens Drive Library item will be placed on the regular agenda for the June 15, 2026 meeting agenda.
