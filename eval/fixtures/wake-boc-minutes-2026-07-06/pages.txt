Title: General Public Comments
Note: Wesley Knott, a resident of northeast Raleigh and Chair of the Wake County Democratic Party, thanked the Board of Commissioners for the opportunity to speak. He shared that he grew up in rural Mississippi, where there was not always an accessible governmental body before which residents could speak. Mr. Knott commented on voter suppression and expressed his appreciation for living in Wake County, stating that the Board has demonstrated its commitment to ensuring every resident has fair and equitable access to the ballot box. He shared that a petition launched two days earlier had already received more than 1,700 signatures from Wake County residents requesting contingent funding to combat voter suppression in Wake County. He voiced support for providing contingent funding to restore the NC State Talley Student Union early voting site. He stated that today was the final opportunity for the Commissioners to express support before early voting sites are finalized.

Anjanette Porter - A Southern Wake County resident referenced comments she had previously submitted through the public comment portal regarding her interactions with Wake County Animal Control and Wake County Public Health following an attack by an unleashed dog. She described an unsatisfactory interaction with Wake County Animal Control Officer Ferguson, who told her to locate the animal that attacked her because the officer did not have the resources to do so. She shared that she was not contacted by a Wake County representative until four days later, at which time she received limited information regarding the rabies protocol. She referenced her written remarks, in which she described experiencing fear, sadness, pain, grief, disbelief, and anger after learning of the potential rabies exposure. She concluded by expressing appreciation to Officer Aaron, Jennifer Brown, and Commissioner Stallings for their efforts to address what she described as shortcomings in Wake County's response.

Dan Fox, MD - Critical Care Physician at WakeMed, provided perspective and support for the merger with WakeMed and Atrium. He shared that the physicians of WakeMed are excited about the partnership. He shared that Atrium is a like-minded partner with a history of collaborating to care for the sickest patients. He shared that physicians at WakeMed have an enduring mission: to care for the people of Wake County. To continue providing excellent care, good intentions and hard work are not enough. He shared that patients will need staff to have access to clinical resources, infrastructure, technology, specialty depth, and capital investment that is required to continue to do the great work. He emphasized the partnership is about ensuring that WakeMed continues to have the resources to remain strong, clinically excellent, and deeply committed to Wake County. He asked the Board of Commissioners to support this strategic partnership.

Pete Tannenbaum - Member of Alliance Medical Ministry. He shared remarks in support of the WakeMed partnership with Atrium. He shared that Alliance Medical Ministry is on the front lines of the uninsured populations of Wake County. He shared there are 75,000 residents in Wake County who do not have health insurance nor access to Medicaid. He shared that WakeMed is a safety-net. He shared that having safety nets is vital to Wake County. He shared that a Wake County needs assessment is performed every three to four years, and 2025 results showed that access to primary care, access to mental health care, and housing are the biggest issues facing Wake County. Alliance Medical Ministry, as a safety-net provider, sees the uninsured population. There are more uninsured persons in the county than in 60% of other counties across North Carolina have in total population. Alliance is a proud partner with WakeMed.

Carolyn Knaup, RN - NW Raleigh, spoke in support of WakeMed and the Atrium strategic combination; sharing that it is not a takeover. She has spent 27+ years as a nurse and is now a member of WakeMed's senior leadership team. In her current role, she develops partnerships for the strategic plan. She has been part of the team for the past 2 years, developing the plan for the strategic combination. The process has been guided by one central goal: strengthening and protecting WakeMed's mission and its vital role in the community. WakeMed and Atrium share strategic priorities and core values. She encouraged and thanked Commissioners for attending and being engaged in community listening sessions and participating in dialogue with WakeMed/Atrium Health leaders. She shared hopes that Commissioners will vote to approve the transfer agreement and the new articles of incorporation.

Waqiah Ellis - Vice President of Nursing department at WakeMed, Raleigh campus. She shared support of the WakeMed and Atrium strategic combination. WakeMed makes a meaningful impact on patients, families, and the community every day. She shared that health care and delivery models are constantly changing and are driven by technology, innovation, and research. WakeMed's strong financial performance allows WakeMed to invest significant capital dollars toward basic needs: to continue to provide high-level care for patients. As technology advances, certain tools will go from being "nice to have" to "necessity" and expected by the community. She shared the strategic combination to address the challenge by providing WakeMed the leverage to access Atrium Health's technology, infrastructure, and experience, which will allow WakeMed to bring accelerated innovations to Wake County. A modern health care environment will be created with tools that improve workflows.

Greg Jarrell, Reverend, lives in Charlotte, invited by members of The Patients Union to share his Atrium experience. He shared that Atrium was formerly established at the Charlotte-Mecklenburg Hospital Authority. He shared concerns of many Charlotte residents. Atrium has grown significantly, while costs have gone up. His organization has been working with its board of commissioners to get to the heart of Atrium (the constitution of Atrium Health's board of directors). He shared an appeal has been submitted to not appoint as many business people who specialize in mergers and acquisitions, but appoint persons with a focus on community care and public health. He shared that the Atrium Health Board slate was returned in 2025. He shared they received full resistance by Atrium to any public accountability or authority that would deny the executive class. He shared that rather than working with Mecklenburg Commissioner Jerrell, Atrium Health refused the input, has operated as though the board slate was pushed through, and Atrium Health has continued to taunt. He shared the Wake County Board of Commissioners' decision is not about quality of care; it is about community input and control. He shared that if the Commissioners vote for what Atrium Health is calling a combination, the Wake County Board of Commissioners will vote away its own power. He shared that if that power is voted away, the Board of Commissioners will not get it back. He cautioned the Wake County Board of Commissioners against the arrangement.

Anthony Pope - Men of Southeast Raleigh, shared the mission of the organization: men who were interested in serving, supporting, and advocating for historically black communities where we were raised and lived. He shared his opposition to the merger between WakeMed and Atrium. He shared that when St. Agnes merged with WakeMed, the delivery of services was seamless. He shared concern that when things are happening in Southeast Raleigh, the community was often left off the agenda. He shared that public meetings are typically held so that a box can be checked and decisions are already made before being shared with the community. He asked how the merger will impact those people who are indigent, noting that the marginalized communities are the ones that will be impacted. He asked that residents in Southeast Raleigh be allowed to have a seat at the table and to be present before the critical decisions are made. He asked the Board of Commissioners to reconsider the takeover as they keep in mind the residents who don't have access to health care. He shared that the high-tech jobs that are coming to Raleigh do not hire residents of Southeast Raleigh.

Heysoll Rodriguez Soto - Wake County resident. She shared that this is about timing. In opposition to the WakeMed Atrium merger. She shared the takeover is being proposed at a moment when patients are already under enormous pressure. She shared healthcare costs are rising, and families are struggling with medical debt. She shared more people are going to need help paying for health care. The WakeMed charity care system is more important now. Atrium Health is trying to present the merger as a positive change. She shared that once the merger is over, will patients have to wait until situations become urgent before healthcare arrives. She shared this is the wrong time for the Commissioners to gamble with patients' healthcare protections. She urged the Commissioners to block the merger and keep WakeMed's safety net in place for Wake County families.

Rob Stephens - shared that the WakeMed Atrium merger is not a routine matter and is the most consequential decision that will be happening in Wake County this year. He shared does not want to have public input. He shared Atrium Health wanted this item on the Board of Commissioners' consent agenda to prevent public input. He shared that Atrium is trying to sell people that charity care is a program with financial assistance. He shared Atrium Health's charity care policy is narrower than what WakeMed currently covers. He shared Atrium Health's charity care excludes care that can be safely postponed. He shared elective does not always mean optional. He shared patients should not have to wait to get needed health care. Currently, WakeMed offers a 62% discount to all uninsured patients over 300% regardless of income, and Atrium Health's prices are higher, which may leave a bigger discount on paper, but still leave patients owing more. He shared the Board of Commissioners is the body to decide if the WakeMed hospital is taken away from residents.

Nathan Foster - Health Policy Specialist with North Carolina Justice Center. He shared opposition to the merger with WakeMed and Atrium. He shared the proposed takeover would raise hospital prices, undermine community control, and threaten WakeMed's quality of care and commitment to residents. He shared the 1997 agreement with WakeMed gives the Commissioners the right to reject the deal. He shared the first impression of Raleigh Mayor Janet Cowell, that WakeMed has a 10% profit margin, a much lower rate of profit, and Atrium Health's profit is 40%. Atrium has promised investments in the community, but not guaranteed. WakeMed is more generous to the community than Atrium will be. He shared in 2022, 40% of patients sued for medical debt were sued by Atrium.

Reverend Hershey Mallette Stephens - WakeMed saved her life when she was pregnant. She spoke on maternal morbidity and how she received care at WakeMed for that diagnosis. Regarding WakeMed being handed over to Atrium, she does not hear growth, care, bigger is better; but what she hears is a warning. She shared researchers from Brown University as raising numerous concerns, called a de facto cross-market merger. She shared that mergers like this have been linked to price increases from 7% - 17%. This increase is the difference between getting the care you need and putting it off.
