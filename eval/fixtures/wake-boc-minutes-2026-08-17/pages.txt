Agenda item: 28.
File number: 16-7288
Type: Regular Item
Title: Business Development Grant Policy Updates
Matter name: Michael James, Assistant County Manager Michael Haley, Wake County Economic Development
Note: Michael James, Assistant County Manager, and Michael Haley, Executive Director and Wake County Economic Development Senior Vice President, shared the presentation.

Mr. James shared the Business Development Grant Policy Recommendations
He shared the Policy Goals and Objectives.
He shared the Policy History.
He shared the Policy Results: Active Agreements.
He shared Wake Pays for Performance.
He shared the "Clawback" Provisions.
He shared Board Discussions.

Michael Haley shared Proposed Changes.
He shared Adjust Wage Thresholds.
He shared Reduce Number of Project Tiers.
He shared Establish Transformational Project Tier.
He shared Decrease State Match Minimum.
He shared Adjust HQ Policy Tier Structure.
He shared Targeted Growth Areas.
He shared Policy Summary with Proposed Changes.

Commissioner Stallings shared that this is a real value to the board and that we should always think about economic opportunities and economic mobility for our residents. She shared that Wake County wants to create as many economic opportunities for residents as they can. She shared that sometimes it is a misconception that the payout is at the front, but the payout does not occur until performance measures are met, and that we do have clawback measures in place to safeguard the dollars that the board is stewards of.

Commissioner Thomas shared that, as Chair of the Economic Development Committee, they have been talking about this for quite some time. She shared that Mr. Haley and Mr. James have been listening to their feedback and have made the tweaks and changed some of the language. She shared that her question is about the adjusted wage thresholds. She shared that when it says the employer promotes economic upward mobility, what is the difference between promoting economic mobility and demonstrating that they already provide economic mobility.

Mr. Haley shared that when they provide the board with project summaries, they want to give real examples from the company. He shared that there is a strong policy and history of supporting economic development in Wake County. He shared that it has a lot to do with their policy and the strength of that, but also when working with these companies, it is often the case that they work with companies for months at a time before bringing an item to the board to talk about incentives at all. He shared that they want to see demonstrated examples and history of the items.

Commissioner Thomas asked if Wake Tech and Capital Area Workforce Development were involved in every conversation around economic mobility. Mr. Haley responded that they are.

Commissioner Evans shared that she feels like they have made the dollars and wage ranges relevant to current times rather than years ago, and that is reflected in the transformational projects as well because that's just a whole scope of project that they did not dream of seven to eight years ago. She shared that when the board is reviewing a potential business that wants to come to this area, and they share that they want to offer a certain number of jobs, a distribution list of jobs is shared by Mr. Haley and Mr. James, so the board has been very focused on economic mobility for years. She shared that Wake County was known for years as a high-tech area, but not everyone benefited from prosperity. She shared that the board looks at the whole spectrum of jobs. She shared that she is proud of all the work done and what we do. She shared that she is happy to support the changes.

Vice-Chair Jackson shared that we have reached a place of flexibility and balance with our values and are positioned to seize what is happening right now. She thanked them for bringing back the active agreement. She asked how they verify or ensure the headquarters strategy.

Mr. Haley shared the appreciation. He shared that they set community expectations on how companies reflect the broader Wake County economy and community. When they work with headquarters, they make sure that they really understand the great array of existing companies headquartered here in Wake County and are long-standing advocates.

Commissioner Adamson shared comments on the priority growth areas. She shared that living wage jobs and barriers are education. Put companies that offer upward mobility in their community; not only are they able to get there, but it cuts down on traffic, and the closer you are to your job, the less you drive to get there. It cuts down on everyone's expenses, especially in a low-income area.
Action text: Commissioner Thomas moved, seconded by Vice-Chair Jackson, that the Board of Commissioners approve the updated Wake County Business Development Grant Policy to establish a Transformational Project Tier, create a job-based payout structure for Headquarters projects, and make adjustments to wage thresholds and the minimum investment for County participation in State Match projects. The motion carried by the following vote:
Action: accepted
Result: Pass
